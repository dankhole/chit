# 100 app names, ranked

For a small, beautiful macOS menu-bar task app: quiet, local-first, attached to the project, and comfortable with human-readable files. Ranked by fit, memorability, sound, and the quality of the association. These are creative candidates; availability has not been checked.

1. **Margin** — The useful space beside the main work. Understated, literary, and an especially good fit for a menu-bar companion.
2. **Redbook** — A nod to Tolkien’s Red Book of Westmarch: the record of an adventure, kept by the people doing it.
3. **Quire** — A gathering of paper sheets. Short, tactile, and distinctive; a lovely metaphor for tasks collected into projects.
4. **Inkling** — Both a small idea and a nod to the Inklings, Tolkien and C. S. Lewis’s literary circle. Warm and inventive.
5. **Samwise** — The dependable companion who helps carry the work. A particularly strong character reference for a quiet project helper.
6. **Dogear** — A small mark that helps you return to something. Bookish, memorable, and naturally suited to unfinished work.
7. **Fieldnote** — Notes made where the work happens. Especially apt for a task file that lives inside its project.
8. **Memento** — Something that helps you remember, with a recognizable film connection built around written reminders.
9. **Folio** — A compact collection of pages. Elegant and native-app sounding, with room for both tasks and project notes.
10. **Sidequest** — The next small adventure beside your main work. Playful, immediately understandable, and comfortably game-adjacent.
11. **Commonplace** — From commonplace books: personal collections of useful passages and ideas. Thoughtful, humane, and grounded in note-taking history.
12. **Docket** — A concise record of matters to handle. Clear task-list meaning, with a little more character than a generic productivity name.
13. **Jotter** — A small notebook for quick entries. Friendly, practical, and easy to say.
14. **Tally** — Keeping track, one mark at a time. Short, approachable, and directly connected to checking things off.
15. **Footnote** — Useful detail beside a larger body of work. A natural metaphor for project notes and optional subtasks.
16. **Zettel** — A nod to Zettelkasten and its individual note slips. Compact, distinctive, and appealing to people who like keeping their own text.
17. **Waybook** — A book for the work ahead. Suggests direction and a project’s continuing story without prescribing a workflow.
18. **Pensieve** — The Harry Potter vessel for stored memories. A strong, recognizable image for putting mental clutter somewhere you can revisit.
19. **Bookend** — A small supporting object that keeps a collection orderly. Particularly fitting for an app that stays beside the main project.
20. **Ibid** — The compact citation meaning “in the same place.” A clever literary fit for tasks stored with their project.
21. **Ariadne** — The mythological thread through the labyrinth. Evokes finding the next step through complicated project work.

## Eight standout directions

| Name | Why it stands out | Tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Margin** | Best overall fit: a useful, unobtrusive place beside the work. Easy to pronounce and visually restrained. | The common word is less distinctive in conversation and search. |
| **Redbook** | Best literary-object reference. A project's own record of the journey fits local ownership especially well. | The Tolkien connection may need explaining; “red” also suggests a color identity. |
| **Quire** | Best compact, bookish name. It sounds like a considered Mac utility and suggests a small collection. | Some people will need the meaning or pronunciation explained; it sounds like “choir.” |
| **Inkling** | Best balance of literary reference and everyday meaning. Small ideas becoming work is a natural product story. | It can sound more like an idea-capture app than a task app. |
| **Samwise** | Best fictional companion. It communicates dependable help more vividly than most abstract productivity names. | It ties the identity closely to Tolkien and makes the app feel like a character. |
| **Dogear** | Best physical-book metaphor. A tiny mark that helps you pick up where you left off feels exactly right. | People may write it as “dog-ear” or “dog ear”; the name is more playful than austere. |
| **Fieldnote** | Best expression of project-local context: the note stays where the actual work takes place. | It suggests observation and note-taking more strongly than completing tasks. |
| **Memento** | Best widely recognizable memory reference. Concise, evocative, and immediately connected to remembering. | The film association is darker and more anxious than the intended calm experience. |

My first shortlist would be **Margin**, **Redbook**, **Quire**, and **Inkling**. If the app should feel like a named companion, **Samwise** is the strongest alternative.
